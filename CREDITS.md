# Credits

Each line: whose work, cite key, where the paper uses it. Checked against the sources on 25 Nov 2026
(MZ24 from the arXiv source of 2408.03921, Zer24 from 2303.16240, McG24 from its arXiv source).

- McGinnis and Zerbib, MZ24, Question 3.16 (quoted verbatim in Section 1) and Theorem 3.15 (the bound
  floor(p/2)+1 in every dimension, credited there to Zerbib). Theorem and question numbers checked by counting
  the shared theorem counter of the survey's Section 3.
- Zerbib, Zer24, Theorem 1.2 (planar bound floor(p/2)+1, tight by the regular (2p+1)-gon) in Section 1;
  Theorem 1.6 (Gallai's theorem as stated there) for T(d,n) <= n.
- Gallai, Gal59: the interval theorem behind T(d,n) <= n (Section 1).
- McGinnis, McG24: the conjecture L(k) = ceil(k/2) (its Section 4) of which the planar bound is a special
  case, as MZ24 records (Section 1). Checked directly: the sets of a C(k) are pairwise disjoint (McG24, after
  the definition), so a family with packing number at most p is C(p+1)-free and the conjecture gives
  ceil((p+1)/2) = floor(p/2)+1 lines, for p >= 3.
- AK95 checked against the paper itself (Princeton preprint pierce4.pdf, abstract: the constant C(p,q,d) for
  compact convex sets with the hyperplane-transversal (p,q) property) and its record on Crossref (DCG 13, issue
  3-4, 245-256, 1995).
- Alon and Kalai, AK95: the (p,q) theorem for hyperplane transversals, p >= q >= d+1 (Section 1); statement
  matches the summary in MZ24, Section 3 (hyperplanes subsection).
- Dol'nikov, Dol92: the transversal theorem, Theorem 2.2 here (Section 2), also stated as FMSS24 Theorem 1.2;
  used in Lemma 2.3 and in the proof of Theorem 1.1 (Section 4).
- Borsuk, Bor33: the Borsuk-Ulam theorem in the proof sketch of Theorem 2.2 (Section 2).
- Frick, Murray, Simon and Stemmler, FMSS24: convex hulls of large packings (Section 1 and the proof of
  Theorem 1.1); Theorem 1.5 (case m = 1) compared with Theorem 1.1 for d = 2^(k-1); Theorem 1.7 (polytopes in
  R^3 with the (8,2) property are pierced by three planes) in Section 1 and in the closing remark.
